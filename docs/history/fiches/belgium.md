### A. Armée active (temps de paix)

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **33 000** |
| Date de référence | January 1914 (peace establishment of 1913-14; Belgium was not mobilised until 2 August 1914) |
| Définition | Approximate peacetime strength actually under arms of the Belgian Army (the conscript active army with the colours plus the permanent cadre): on the order of 30,000-35,000 officers and men, taken here as about 33,000. Under the 1909 law the term with the colours was short (roughly 12-15 months by arm), so the standing force was essentially the annual contingent plus cadres. This figure EXCLUDES the Garde Civique (civil guard, a separate, only partly trained citizen militia numbering roughly 40,000-50,000 on paper) and the trained reserve. It is NOT the August 1914 mobilised field army (c.117,000-120,000) nor the total mobilised forces (c.200,000-300,000). |
| Source | `SEC_west_SYB1914` — Statesman's Year-Book, 1914 edition (ed. J. Scott Keltie), national entries for Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg and Switzerland, sections 'Defence' / 'Army'. The annual compilation of official government returns closest in date to January 1914; the standard reference for peace establishments of the European armies. Used here for order-of-magnitude and structure; exact figures could not be re-verified in this environment (see METHODOLOGY). |
| Page / passage | Belgium, 'Defence — Army'; see also EB1911_BE, article 'Belgium', section 'Defence' |
| Lien | — |
| Fiabilité | moyenne |
| Calcul | ESTIMATE. The standard references agree on the 1909 conscription framework and on a small professional cadre, but do not yield a verifiable daily return for January 1914. An annual contingent of c.25,000-30,000 conscripts serving roughly 12-15 months, plus c.4,000-6,000 long-service officers, NCOs and specialists, implies c.30,000-35,000 men under arms at any one time; the mid value 33,000 is adopted and explicitly flagged as an estimate. |

### B. Réserves mobilisables

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **100 000** |
| Date de référence | 1913-14 reserve organisation; trained reservists available on mobilisation in January 1914 |
| Définition | ESTIMATED trained reservists: men who had completed their term with the colours and remained liable for recall, available to bring the field army and the fortress garrisons up to war strength. With an annual intake of c.25,000-30,000 and a multi-year reserve liability, the trained reserve pool was on the order of 100,000. These men were NOT under arms in January 1914. The partly trained Garde Civique is excluded here (listed as a divergence). |
| Source | `SEC_west_SYB1914` — Statesman's Year-Book, 1914 edition (ed. J. Scott Keltie), national entries for Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg and Switzerland, sections 'Defence' / 'Army'. The annual compilation of official government returns closest in date to January 1914; the standard reference for peace establishments of the European armies. Used here for order-of-magnitude and structure; exact figures could not be re-verified in this environment (see METHODOLOGY). |
| Page / passage | Belgium, 'Defence — Army' (reserve and territorial/Garde Civique provisions) |
| Lien | — |
| Fiabilité | moyenne |
| Calcul | ESTIMATE by order of magnitude: c.25,000-30,000 trained men per annual class multiplied by roughly 3-4 years of reserve liability gives c.75,000-120,000; 100,000 is adopted as a round mid estimate. No exact contemporary return is available here. |

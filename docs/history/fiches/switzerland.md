### A. Armée active (temps de paix)

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **12 000** |
| Date de référence | January 1914 (peacetime militia training cycle; no standing army and no mobilisation until August 1914) |
| Définition | Switzerland had NO standing army. The only troops actually under arms in peacetime were the small permanent instructional cadre (roughly 2,500-3,000 officers, NCOs and specialists) plus conscripts undergoing recruit school (Rekrutenschule) and men attending refresher courses (Wiederholungskurse). Averaged over the training year, on the order of 10,000-15,000 men were under arms at any one time; 12,000 is adopted here as a mid ESTIMATE. This is strictly distinct from the theoretical militia establishment (c.200,000 trained men, see reserves) and from the August 1914 mobilised army (c.200,000-250,000). |

Reliable for structure; simultaneous under-arms headcounts are not given directly. |

### B. Réserves mobilisables

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **190 000** |
| Date de référence | 1914 militia establishment; trained reservists available on mobilisation |
| Définition | ESTIMATED trained reservists: the great body of Swiss males who had passed through recruit training and were enrolled in the active levy (Auszug), the Landwehr and the Landsturm, but who were NOT under arms in peacetime. With a total mobilisable force of roughly 200,000-250,000 in 1914 and only about 12,000 continuously under arms, the trained reserve pool was on the order of 190,000. Under the Swiss system this reserve WAS the army in wartime; in January 1914 its members were civilians pursuing their ordinary occupations. |
| Source | `SEC_west_EB1911_CH` — Encyclopaedia Britannica, 11th edition (1911), article 'Switzerland', section 'Defence'. Describes the Swiss militia system: no standing army, recruit schools and refresher courses, the active levy / Landwehr / Landsturm, the small permanent instructional cadre and the large trained manpower pool. Reliable for structure; simultaneous under-arms headcounts are not given directly. |
| Page / passage | article 'Switzerland', section 'Defence' (active levy, Landwehr, Landsturm and total trained strength) |
| Lien | — |
| Fiabilité | moyenne |
| Calcul | ESTIMATE by subtraction: c.200,000 mobilisable (standard literature, total first-line) minus c.12,000 simultaneously under arms = c.190,000. Both inputs are approximate; the value is explicitly an estimate and refers to trained men available, not to troops present with the colours in January 1914. |

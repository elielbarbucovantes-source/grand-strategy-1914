| Définition | Permanent troops under arms: the small standing corps of officers, the 20-man perianiki bodyguard and the permanently embodied militia cadres; Montenegro had NO standing army in the modern sense |
| Source | `SEC_balkans_EB1911_MONTENEGRO` — 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica, vol. 18, article 'Montenegro' by James David Bourchier, section 'Defence': 'There is a standing corps of officers, but no standing army'; 11 infantry brigades, 56 battalions; 'The war strength is estimated at from 38,000 to 42,000 men' (first ban ~32,000, second ban/reserve 5,000-6,000, artillery ~1,500); 20,000-22,000 men of military age absent abroad; 'About 20,000 men can concentrate at a given spot within 48 hours'; permanent corps of 20 perianiki (1907); arsenal at Spuzh. |

| Fiabilité | faible |
| Calcul | EB1911 is explicit: 'There is a standing corps of officers, but no standing army.' Permanent formations were tiny (20 perianiki in 1907; the 500-man Cettigne and Podgoritza permanent battalions and the Nikšić artillery brigade were ABOLISHED in 1905 for economy). All young men performed only 12 days' obligatory training annually at local centres. The 3,000 value is therefore an ORDER-OF-MAGNITUDE ESTIMATE of officers, NCOs and trainees actually under arms at any one time in January 1914, NOT a documented return. |

### B. Réserves mobilisables

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **35 000** |
| Date de référence | 1914-01 (highly uncertain estimate) |
| Définition | Trained militia (first ban and second ban) theoretically callable - i.e. all able-bodied males who had passed the 12-day annual training |
| Source | `SEC_balkans_EB1911_MONTENEGRO` — 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica, vol. 18, article 'Montenegro' by James David Bourchier, section 'Defence': 'There is a standing corps of officers, but no standing army'; 11 infantry brigades, 56 battalions; 'The war strength is estimated at from 38,000 to 42,000 men' (first ban ~32,000, second ban/reserve 5,000-6,000, artillery ~1,500); 20,000-22,000 men of military age absent abroad; 'About 20,000 men can concentrate at a given spot within 48 hours'; permanent corps of 20 perianiki (1907); arsenal at Spuzh. |
| Page / passage | article 'Montenegro', section 'Defence' |
| Lien | https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Montenegro |
| Fiabilité | faible |
| Calcul | EB1911: war strength estimated at 38,000-42,000 (infantry ~32,000 first ban + 5,000-6,000 second ban, artillery ~1,500), with 20,000-22,000 military-age men absent abroad; about 20,000 could concentrate within 48 hours. Subtracting the small permanent cadre gives roughly 35,000 trained reservists. After the Balkan Wars Montenegro gained Plav, Gusinje and part of the Sandžak, increasing population and nominal manpower, but no reliable post-1913 return was located. Treat this number as a ROUGH ORDER OF MAGNITUDE ONLY. |

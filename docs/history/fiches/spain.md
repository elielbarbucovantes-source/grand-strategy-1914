| Valeur retenue | **128 073** |
| Date de référence | 1911-1913 peace establishment, as published in 1913; valid approximation for January 1914 (conscription law of 29 June 1911 still in force; no general mobilisation before July 1914) |
| Définition | Official PEACE ESTABLISHMENT of the Spanish Army: 13,405 officers + 114,668 men = 128,073. This is an establishment/peacetime under-arms figure, explicitly INCLUDING the Guardia Civil (constabulary, 'somewhat less than 20,000', 2,000 mounted) and the Carabineros (military customs police, 'nearly 15,000', 500 mounted). It is NOT the wartime field army and NOT the number of effectives actually present in barracks on any given day. Spain stayed on a peacetime footing through 1914-1918. |

### B. Réserves mobilisables

| Champ | Contenu |
|---|---|
| Valeur retenue | **90 000** |
| Date de référence | 1913 peace organisation; the trained reserve available on mobilisation in January 1914 |
| Définition | Organised SECOND-LINE (reserve) troops: cadres for 116 battalions, 14 squadrons and 14 batteries, intended to form 7 reserve divisions of about 13,000 combatants each — total about 90,000. These are trained reservists who would be called out on mobilisation; they were NOT present under arms in peacetime. Distinct from the c.220,000-combatant first-line wartime field army and from the unorganised 'territorial reserve'. |
| Source | `SEC_iberia_SYB1913` — The Statesman's Year-Book: Statistical and Historical Annual of the States of the World for the Year 1913. Spain: 'Defence — Army', pp. 1260-1262; Portugal: 'Defence', pp. 1151-1152. Figures 'revised after official returns'. |
| Page / passage | Spain, 'Defence — Army', printed p.1261 (Wikisource djvu page 1383) |
| Lien | https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Statesman%27s_Year-Book_1913.djvu/1383 (Spain p.1261); https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Statesman%27s_Year-Book_1913.djvu/1274 (Portugal p.1152); full scan: https://archive.org/details/statesmansyearbo1913newy |
| Fiabilité | haute |
| Calcul | 7 divisions x about 13,000 combatants = about 90,000, as stated directly in the source. Under the 1911 law the total liability was 18 years (3 active, generally reduced to 2; 3 first reserve; 6 second reserve; remainder territorial reserve), so the total trained manpower pool was larger than the 90,000 organised second-line, but the source gives no number for it. |
